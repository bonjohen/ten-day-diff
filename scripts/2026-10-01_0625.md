## Technical - AI/software

First, AI and software.

OpenAI held DevDay 2026 on September 29th.

The headline model is GPT-6.1 Sol, pitched as close to their top Astra model on agentic coding at about a fifth of the token cost.

There's also an Ultrafast tier, up to 300 tokens a second in Codex, and up to 6 times faster in the API.

The parts you'll care about are further down the list.

The Agents API now does computer use.

A new Decisions API answers questions with a fixed set of allowed answers, which is basically structured classification as a product.

And plugins can now subscribe to MCP events, so an outside system can kick off an automation.

That last one is the same idea as event-driven triggers in your own pipeline, and it's worth watching whether Anthropic answers it.

Also, on September 30th, the White House announced what it called "morally binding" safety commitments with Anthropic, Google, Meta, NVIDIA, OpenAI and xAI.

The four-point plan covers internal controls on model capabilities and alignment, plus third-party audits reviewed by a board-level committee.

There's no enforcement mechanism.

For you, it mostly matters as context.

Any vendor questionnaire asking about AI governance will start citing it.

## Technical - Materials science

Materials science.

A Nature paper published September 30th reports wafer-scale epitaxial growth of p-type boron carbon nitride, from groups at the University of Tokyo, National Taiwan University, and National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University.

The trick is a dual precursor: monomethyl ammonia borane plus ammonia borane, which fixes the timing mismatch in how boron, carbon and nitrogen arrive at the surface.

Transistors hit a hole mobility of 100 square centimeters per volt-second and an on/off ratio of 10 to the eighth.

Two-dimensional CMOS has always had a decent n-type story and a weak p-type one.

This is a credible p-type candidate that grows at wafer scale.

And from September 23rd, Nature covered a new boron allotrope called Imma-B60, reported in Nature Chemistry by a team at Yanshan University.

They pressed boron with sodium at high pressure, then baked out the sodium at 900 degrees Celsius under vacuum. What's left conducts more than a million times better than ordinary boron and stretches 23% before breaking.

The ductility comes from the voids the sodium left behind, which let dislocations slip.

It's the first new boron phase since 2009.

## DIY electronics

DIY electronics.

On September 30th, CNX covered the Waveshare ESP32-C5-Touch-LCD-3.5.

It's a 480 by 320 IPS touchscreen board on the ESP32-C5, so dual-band Wi-Fi 6 plus Zigbee and Thread. It has 8 MB of PSRAM, 32 of flash, a mic and an ES8311 codec, a temperature and humidity sensor, a 6-axis IMU, and options for a small camera and a battery.

About $32 to $36 direct.

That's a cheap, self-contained, Matter-capable panel.

Same day, OpenArm 2.0 from Enactic in Tokyo. It's an open-source 7-axis arm with quasi-direct-drive joints, a 606 mm reach, about 4 kilos of nominal payload, 1 kHz CAN FD control and an in-hand camera. Hardware is CERN Open Hardware licensed. Software is Apache 2.0 with ROS 2, MuJoCo and Isaac Lab support.

Not cheap at $6,500, but it's a real teleoperation and imitation learning platform you can fully inspect.

And on September 28th, Up2Stream spun off from Arylic as its own DIY audio brand.

Streamer boards with AirPlay and Spotify Connect, an ESS Sabre DAC board, amplifier boards, and HTTP, UART and TCP control APIs.

Handy if you ever want to wire speakers into home automation.

## Data regulation

Data regulation.

One item.

On September 30th, Governor Newsom signed SB 690.

This follows SB 690, presented September 28th, when it had passed the legislature.

It ends private lawsuits under the California Invasion of Privacy Act's pen register provision for website and app tracking. Only the Attorney General can bring those now.

It applies to pending claims filed within two years of the January 1, 2027 operative date.

It does not touch wiretap claims under Section 631, and the broad commercial purpose exemption was dropped.

For your GTM stack, pixel and tracker exposure under the pen register theory mostly goes away, but session replay and chat widget wiretap suits are still live.

Nothing else new in this area that the last two episodes didn't already cover.

## Claude for GTM engineering

Claude for GTM engineering.

On September 30th, Anthropic made Claude for Government generally available in a FedRAMP High environment.

It includes the desktop app, Claude Code, Skills, Plugins and Projects, with early access to the Claude Code CLI and Claude for Microsoft 365.

The pricing model is interesting. No seat fees, usage-based billing with hard spending caps per tier.

If you sell into the public sector, this is the procurement-ready path.

If any of your HubSpot or Gong enrichment jobs still pin a Sonnet 4.5 model ID, that's a two-month clock.

Swap it and rerun your evals now.

Last, Claude Code 2.1.286, released September 30th.

New pieces beyond what we covered last time.

A DISABLE_WEB_FETCH environment variable turns off the WebFetch tool, which is useful for locked-down headless runs.
