## Technical - AI/software

Strata showed up on Hackaday on October 4th.

It is an open-source runtime from a developer who goes by Niko1221, and it runs a 125 billion parameter model on a gaming PC.

The model is Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a mixture-of-experts design with about 24,000 experts, of which only 10 fire per token.

Strata treats the graphics card's memory as a cache. Hot experts stay on the GPU, the rest sit in system RAM, and a 29 gigabyte lookup table lives on the SSD.

On an RTX 5070 with 12 GB of video memory, the author reports 50 to 90 tokens per second.

It also serves OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs on localhost. So John, you could point an existing client at it for offline tests without touching a billing account.

Two Snowflake changes.

On October 1st, migration of Openflow generation 1 deployments to generation 2 went generally available.

A deployment and all of its runtimes convert together from the Openflow interface, and they come out as schema-scoped objects under normal role-based access control. No deadline was given, and deployments you leave alone keep running.

Check the connector badges before you move, because the Postgres and MySQL change data capture connectors on generation 2 are still in preview.

Then on October 2nd, Snowflake added observability rules for Cortex Agents that run inside native apps.

Snowflake now redacts the provider's orchestration content before it writes the consumer's event table. Providers get agent telemetry at a metadata level by default, and the consumer has to approve a separate specification before message content is shared.

If you ever package an agent as a native app, that is the line between what you see and what the customer sees.

Last in this area, a blog post from cryptographer Matthew Green on September 30th, asking whether sandboxing is enough to contain rogue agents.

His answer is no.

He argues the likely failure is not an agent breaking out, but well-behaved agents obeying instructions that other agents left in shared caches and channels.

He compares it to a worm more than a prison break.

That is worth a look at any pipeline where one agent's output becomes another agent's input.

## Technical - Materials science

The primary research feeds I could read were mostly covered in earlier episodes, so two of these three are reviews.

On September 29, Nature Reviews Materials published a review of spectrally engineered textiles for personal thermoregulation, led by Ronghui Wu at Nanyang Technological University with Po-Chun Hsu's group.

The idea is to manage body heat by tuning how a fabric handles sunlight and mid-infrared radiation. Cooling fabrics reflect sunlight and let body heat radiate through.

Heating fabrics block that infrared. The materials range from nanoporous polyethylene to silk, MXenes and the conducting polymer PEDOT:PSS.

The authors name weak spectral selectivity, unreliable tuning, and manufacturing scale as the open problems.

On October 2nd, the same journal ran a review of magnetic soft materials for medicine from Li Zhang's group at the Chinese University of Hong Kong.

It covers ferrofluids, magnetorheological elastomers, liquid metal composites and magnetic hydrogels, and it sorts devices into wearable, ingestible, implantable, and interventional.

A good map if you want the current state of untethered soft actuators.

The one research paper is in Nature Materials, September 29, from Peking University.

They mask gene therapy viruses so they stay inert until triggered.

They encode an unnatural amino acid carrying a tetrazine group into the capsid, then click on a blocking group through a linker that can be cut. Depending on the linker, a liver protease, near-infrared light or reactive oxygen species removes the mask. In mice, it worked in liver, muscle, brain, and injured heart tissue. The chemistry is the interesting part: a surface that is switched on by its environment.

## DIY electronics

From Hackaday on October 3rd.

Mike Ogrinz reverse engineered the AMT630A, the display chip inside cheap automotive backup camera monitors.

It combines a video switch, an on-screen display generator, and an 8052 core. He could find no documentation, so he skipped the factory firmware and drove the chip over I2C from an ESP32 using test pads on the board.

He published an Arduino library on GitHub.

The result is a text and graphics overlay on composite video from a monitor that costs very little.

October 2nd, a builder named Milen posted what may be the smallest internet radio.

It is an ESP32-C3 dev board, a PCM5102A I2S converter, and a small amplifier on a custom board.

The connectors are bigger than the electronics.

A good reference if you need networked audio in a tiny space.

Also October 2nd, an electronic busy board for a toddler built around a PIC18F67K40.

It uses a two-layer board, multiplexed LEDs, and timer- and interrupt-driven firmware so that three AAA cells last. It is a clean example of low-power design on an 8-bit part.

## Data regulation

Four items.

On October 1st, Judge James Robart in the Western District of Washington dismissed federal wiretap claims against Zillow and Microsoft over Microsoft's Clarity session replay tool.

The ruling is procedural.

The plaintiff's amended complaint went beyond what the court had allowed.

It still ends a closely watched session replay case, and it lands the same week California narrowed pen register suits with SB 690, presented October 1st.

Also October 1st, Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission proposed the conditions for using personal data without consent for AI development and statistics.

Both the provider and the recipient would have to publish the intended use, the scope of the data, and a contact on designated websites, and recipients could not use the data for anything else.

The amended law is expected to take effect around early 2028.

On September 30th, the United Kingdom's Information Commissioner's Office was formally replaced by a board-led Information Commission.

Union members protested on the first day over workplace culture, and a review is pending.

If you deal with the UK regulator, the name and the governance have changed.

And on October 2nd, Hamburg's data protection commissioner said OpenAI's plan for ads in ChatGPT in Europe carries substantial privacy risks.

As reported, users who consent would be profiled on past and current chats plus device and usage data.

Users who do not consent would still get ads based on the current chat.

Expect that second part to be tested under GDPR.

The Models API now returns a field called line on both the list and the single-model endpoints.

It gives the model line, such as Opus, and it is null for a model with no line.
