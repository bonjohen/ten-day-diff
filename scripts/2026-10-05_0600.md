## Technical - AI/software

Google has paused its Open Source Software Vulnerability Rewards Program. The pause took effect October 1st, and TechCrunch reported it October 4th.

Google's stated reason is a significant rise in automated submissions, the vast majority of which are not valid.

An update is promised for the first quarter of 2027. For you, the point is less about security and more about intake.

Any queue that pays per submission is now a target for generated volume.

The same pressure applies to inbound lead forms and support inboxes, so scoring has to happen before a human reads anything.

Databricks shipped three things worth knowing.

On October 1st, cross-engine attribute-based access control went generally available, so row filters and column masks defined in Unity Catalog are now enforced when an outside engine reads a managed table. On October 2nd, JDBC connections in Unity Catalog went GA, and a small pay-per-token decision model called OpenJev, built on a 4 billion parameter Qwen model, appeared on Unity Gateway.

That is the fourth vendor in a week to offer one of these small structured-answer models, after AWS and Cloudflare.

On the Snowflake side, September 30th.

DCM Projects can now define semantic views, generally available, and can attach masking policies in preview.

In plain terms, your semantic layer, with its tables, relationships and metrics, can live in version-controlled definition files and deploy like any other object.

If you are feeding pipeline metrics to an agent, that is the cleaner way to keep the definitions honest.

## Technical - Materials science

Two results.

First, Oregon State, published October 4 in the Journal of the American Chemical Society.

Kyriakos Stylianou's group made a metal-organic framework called BVR-19 that produces hydrogen from water under light, without a platinum-group co-catalyst. The active piece is a sulfur-to-sulfur bond in the organic linker.

Light breaks it temporarily, and the reactive sulfur species that result do the catalytic work. The release gives no efficiency number, so treat it as a mechanism paper for now.

The interesting idea is putting the catalytic site in the linker instead of the metal node.

Second, the Max Planck Institute for the Structure and Dynamics of Matter, in Nature Physics, October 2nd.

They drove superconductors with electrical pulses a few picoseconds long, launched from photoconductive switches.

Vortices and heating are too slow to respond on that timescale, so the samples carried current well past the usual critical current and approached the depairing limit, where Cooper pairs themselves break. Niobium nitride, an s-wave material, failed abruptly at a sharp threshold.

YBCO, which is d-wave, weakened gradually. So the shape of the failure reads out the gap symmetry, using a transport measurement instead of spectroscopy.

## DIY electronics

All three are from Hackaday on October 4th.

A builder named Vladimir made a working laptop around an ATmega2560, an 8-bit AVR.

It runs FreeRTOS with a custom driver for a 128 by 64 monochrome LCD, and it plays video at 24 frames per second with sound, plus games, notes and task switching.

The source is not public yet.

The lesson is in the display driver.

Most of the speed came from skipping the stock library.

BlobJump is a Raspberry Pi game for kids where the controller is a belt.

Each belt holds an ESP32-C3, an MPU-6050 motion sensor, and a rechargeable battery, and jumping or ducking drives the on-screen character.

It is a cheap, complete template for a wireless motion node.

Les' Lab added a passive Q-switch, a chromium-doped YAG crystal, to a neodymium YAG laser pulled from a tattoo-removal machine.

Before: 137 millijoules in 150 microseconds, about 1 kilowatt peak. After: 77 millijoules in 14 nanoseconds, 1.3 megawatts peak.

Less energy per shot, a thousand times the peak power.

It can ionize air, but the pulses are too short to cut metal well.

A nice bench demonstration of a saturable absorber.

## Data regulation

India has blocked Bitchat, Jack Dorsey's Bluetooth mesh messaging app.

On October 3rd, it disappeared from Apple's App Store and Google Play in India, and its website went dark across Indian internet providers.

The order came under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act. The Internet Freedom Foundation calls it unconstitutional, arguing that 69A covers content, not an app's function.

It is a rare case of a state blocking a messaging tool for how it works, not for what was said on it.

In the US, on October 4, the president announced a Super Intelligence Force.

This follows the Super Intelligence executive order, presented October 4. It is a federal task force chaired by Jay Clayton, with FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson as a vice chair, and it has 120 days to report on risks and opportunities.

The charter stresses avoiding overregulation.

Ferguson's seat matters because his FTC is the one currently probing agent developers.

And in Italy, on October 2nd, the Garante fined IQVIA 7 million euros.

The company treated a health database as anonymous.

The regulator found the coded records could be linked to about a million patients, because detailed records plus a stable patient code let individuals be singled out. Hashing an email or swapping in an ID does not take data out of GDPR.

That applies directly to any enrichment or identity resolution table you build.

## Claude for GTM engineering

No new Claude Code release since 2.1.289, so three items from around it.

HubSpot posted on September 28 that Claude for Small Business now has five growth workflows that run on the HubSpot connector on all paid Claude plans.

They are slash commands.

Monday Brief, Speed to Lead, Grow Pipeline, CRM Autopilot and Growth Pulse. Speed to Lead takes an inbound inquiry, scores fit, drafts a reply with meeting times, and creates the contact.

By default, Claude waits for approval before anything goes out.

These are close to what you build by hand, so read them as reference designs, and as the new baseline a small customer gets for free.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 reached Snowflake Cortex in public preview on September 30.

This follows the Sonnet 5.5 launch, presented September 30th.

It is available in Cortex AI Functions and the Cortex REST API, with cross-region inference turned on, so you can score or summarize Gong and HubSpot rows where they already sit.

Last, a study from the marketing firm Graphite, reported October 1st.

They had models rewrite 10,000 pre-ChatGPT articles and compared phrase frequencies. Opus 5.5 uses 99% fewer em dashes than Opus 5, but it says "this matters" 116 times more often than the human sample, and "dependable" 23 times more often.

If Claude drafts your outbound, add those phrases to your lint list.
