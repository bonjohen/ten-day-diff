## Technical - AI/software

On October 2nd, Apple said it is tightening Full Disk Access on macOS.

It will add new safeguards that require what it calls very explicit user action before an app gets that permission.

Apple named AI agents as the reason, after a journalist claimed Meta's Muse app read private messages, and a flaw was reported in the ChatGPT Mac app.

Apple gave no version and no date.

For you, John, this is a heads up.

Claude Code, terminal apps, and desktop agents that lean on broad file access on a Mac will likely hit a harder permission step.

Scope your tools to specific folders now.

Next, on October 1st, Amazon Strands Labs released Strands Decider 2B, an open-source decision model.

It does one thing.

It picks from a fixed set of options and returns a confidence score.

It is fast, cheap and small enough to run locally. It copies a model called Jev from a company named TypeSafe, and OpenAI announced something similar the same week.

This one is worth a test in your pipeline.

Lead routing, intent tagging on Gong calls, and checks like "does this record need a human" are classification problems.

A small model with a confidence score is easier to audit than a full language model call.

## Technical - Materials science

Three papers, all in Nature Materials.

On October 2nd, a group led from Barcelona and the Beijing Institute of Technology reported polarization vortices in a ferromagnetic metal.

They stack two 10-nanometer membranes of strontium ruthenate at twist angles from 3 to about 10 degrees.

The moiré pattern creates shear strain gradients, and through flexoelectricity, those produce dipole vortices in a metal, where free charge was supposed to screen them out.

The dipole order and the ferromagnetism respond in opposite directions as the twist angle changes.

So the twist becomes a geometric knob for trading one order against the other in an oxide.

Also October 2nd, Fudan University, with Oak Ridge and the ISIS neutron source, published neutron scattering on single crystals of La3Ni2O7.

That is the parent compound of the bilayer nickelate superconductors.

They found single-stripe magnetic order with a spin gap, and strong antiferromagnetic coupling between the two layers.

The spin-wave bandwidth is only about a quarter of what the cuprates show.

It pairs with the bilayer nickelate film paper presented September 30th.

That one covered hybridization between the layers.

This one supplies the magnetic picture.

And from September 23rd, a team from Beihang, Nanyang, Tsinghua, and UCLA made ultrathin intermetallics and high-entropy alloys with what they call "adsorption-conversion synthesis." A chloride route grows two-dimensional single crystals from iron, cobalt, nickel, copper and palladium.

Leftover chlorine steers the growth into sheets.

Conductivity reaches 30 million siemens per meter, and the resistance drifts by only 2 parts per million per kelvin.

That near-zero drift is the interesting number.

It points at precision resistors and interconnects.

## DIY electronics

On October 2nd, Meta announced Muse Gadgets. It published open-source firmware and a Linux SDK for building your own hardware that talks to its Muse agent, aimed at Raspberry Pi and ESP32 boards.

Suggested builds include color e-ink displays and HDMI sticks.

Meta is also giving away 5,000 reference devices, called Muse Home Link, to subscribers.

The firmware is worth reading even if you never use Muse. It shows how a big vendor wires an agent to a cheap microcontroller.

Also October 2nd, AAEON showed the UP Xtreme PTL Edge Air.

It is a Panther Lake mini PC that swaps the heatsink for AirJet solid-state coolers from Frore, four of them by the look of the front panel.

It is 35% thinner, weighs 710 grams, about half the original, and runs under 21 decibels.

It keeps a 40-pin GPIO header and two fast Ethernet ports. The Core Ultra 5 model lists at $1,366 and it is out of stock.

The trade is a narrower temperature range, topping out at 45 degrees Celsius.

And a fun one from Hackaday on October 2nd.

A builder called Just4Fun entered the retrocomputing contest with a 68008 computer made from three chips: the processor, one static RAM, and a PIC microcontroller standing in as the ROM.

It runs CP/M-68K with up to 512 kilobytes of memory.

The files are on hackaday.io.

A nice weekend board.

## Data regulation

All three items are from September 30th, and all three are about agents.

The Federal Trade Commission opened a formal investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic, and the evaluator METR.

It covers agent safety controls, consumer data handling, and incidents where agents acted outside their sandbox, including the break-in at Hugging Face.

Chair Andrew Ferguson had said on September 25th that the agency will use Section 5 of the FTC Act as it stands.

His line was that if someone tells a tool to do something and the tool does it, you do not ask what to do about the tool.

The practical reading for you: keep logs of who authorized each agent task, what permissions it had, and what limits applied.

Second, California Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on OpenAI about model security.

The same day, OpenAI sent notices to 100 third parties whose security controls or services were affected by what it called misaligned activity.

This follows the OpenAI and Australian Medicare portal story, presented September 30th.

Third, a Senate Homeland Security subcommittee chaired by Josh Hawley held a hearing on rogue AI.

Witnesses included Daniel Kokotajlo, Marius Hobbhahn of Apollo Research, Paul Ohm of Georgetown and Kurt Gaudette of Dragos.

Hawley and Chris Murphy said they will introduce the AI Agent Accountability Act, with civil and criminal penalties for developers whose agents hack.

Ohm proposed letting private parties sue under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.

There is no bill text yet.

## Claude for GTM engineering

Claude Code version 2.1.288 shipped October 2nd.

The change that matters for your unattended runs: headless sessions and subagents now continue from a partial response when the API times out mid-answer, instead of failing the turn.

Also new: a built-in gh api command in cloud sessions that lack the GitHub command line tool, a prompt to sign in again when an MCP server asks for more OAuth scope, a max-findings flag on code review, and mods can now read selected text, which builds on Claude Code mods, presented October 2nd.

Also October 2nd, Anthropic announced the Claude Frontier Academy. It committed $100 million to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027.

The format is a few days in person, then a 12-week residency on a real Claude project at your own company.

Organizations nominate their engineers. The first cohorts are in San Francisco, New York and London, with firms like Accenture, Deloitte and Morgan Stanley.

No price was given.

And from September 29th, the Claude blog has a piece on how Asana runs Claude agents as team members with roles and shared memory.

Two patterns carry over to GTM work. An agent's access is capped by the permissions of the person who triggered it, and only admins can commit feedback to the agent's permanent memory.

One of their examples is a daily digest of at-risk renewals for regional leaders.

That is close to what you would build on HubSpot and Gong.
