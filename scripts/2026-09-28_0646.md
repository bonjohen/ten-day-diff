A new Opus model that costs less than the one it replaces. Plastic bags turned into diesel. And a California bill that could shut off thousands of pixel-tracking lawsuits, if the governor signs it by Wednesday. This is The Ten-Day Diff.

## Models and platforms

Start with the model race, because the last ten days were busy.

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5 on September 22. The headline is price. It performs at roughly Fable 5.1 level on most work, but it runs about 40% cheaper than Opus 5. List pricing is $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, and cache reads drop to twenty cents. You get a million-token context window and 128,000 tokens of output, with adaptive thinking always on. There's also a new beta that lets you inject tools mid-conversation through system messages, which matters if you build agents that pick up capabilities as they go.

Same day, OpenAI released two GPT-6 variants, Luna and Sol. A day earlier, Xiaomi put out MiMo V2.6, in Pro and Flash versions, so the open-weights side kept moving too.

One smaller item for API people. Cache diagnostics is now generally available, so the beta header is gone. And if you run Managed Agents, you can now restrict which domains their web search and fetch tools can reach. That's a quiet change, but a real one for anyone who has to answer a security review.

## Materials

Over to the lab bench.

Oak Ridge published a surprisingly simple way to turn polyethylene into gasoline and diesel range fuels. That's the plastic in shopping bags and cutting boards, and the catalyst is cheap and aluminum-based. Simple and cheap is the part to watch, because most plastic upcycling chemistry dies on catalyst cost.

At Stanford, researchers watched single phonons, quanta of sound, jump between energy states in real time. That's a first. The applications are down the road, in quantum error correction and sensing, but the measurement itself is the news.

In photovoltaics, a Nature paper showed large perovskite modules passivated with lead carboxylates. They held up well, and outperformed commercial silicon modules on yield. Durability has always been perovskite's weak spot, so this is worth tracking.

Last one. Nature Materials reported microwave-to-optical conversion in CrSBr (chromium sulfide bromide), a layered antiferromagnet, driven by coupling between magnons and excitons. If you care about connecting superconducting qubits to optical networks, that's a candidate platform.

## On the bench

For the DIY crowd.

LILYGO updated its USB dongle with an ESP32-C5. You now get dual-band WiFi 6, 16 MB of flash and 8 MB of PSRAM, and a Qwiic connector, with microSD built into the USB plug.

If you want thermal imaging at the edge, look at the 8Sight T100. It pairs a 320×240 long-wave infrared sensor at 60 frames per second with an STM32N6, whose NPU is rated at 600 GOPS. It runs your own models fully offline.

Two software picks. MemBrowse tracks firmware RAM and flash usage on every commit in CI, and it's free for open-source projects. Anyone who's watched an image quietly outgrow its flash will get why that matters. And KryonOS turns a touchscreen ESP32 into a small JavaScript computer with its own app store.

Quick hits. A dual GMSL2 camera hat for the Pi 5, Brainchip's AKD1500 edge AI cards starting at $99, and an all-in-one NEMA-17 servo from Gearotons.

## Privacy watch

Regulation, and there's a deadline this week.

California SB 690 is on Governor Newsom's desk, and he has until September 30. It removes the private right of action for pen-register claims over website and app tracking, leaving only the Attorney General able to sue. It also reaches back to cases filed since January 1, 2025. That covers a big share of the pixel-lawsuit wave. It does not touch the wiretap or recording provisions, though, so expect plaintiffs to shift their theories rather than go away. One compliance vendor is already calling it law. Check the signing status yourself before you rely on that.

In Europe, a leaked Council draft of the Digital Omnibus would let companies process personal data for AI training and operation under existing legal bases, with thin safeguards. Civil society groups are pushing hard against it, especially the proposed changes to pseudonymisation.

Colorado's Chatbot Safety Act is in rulemaking. It covers age assurance, persistent AI disclosure, and self-harm response protocols. Comments are open until October 26, and the law takes effect January 1.

Federally, the IAPP now counts 49 distinct privacy proposals in Congress. Still no omnibus law.

## GTM engineer's corner

Last segment, for people who build revenue tooling on Claude.

Salesforce in Claude is in beta. It brings accounts, opportunities, and pipeline into Claude, with 37 prebuilt sales skills.

Claude Tag, which is Claude in Slack, now supports personal connectors. You can ask for your own open deals or your own calendar inside a shared channel, and it runs under your permissions, not the channel's. You can review each response before it posts, or let auto mode handle it. It's on Team plans now, with Enterprise to follow.

The biggest one for builders is the new plugin directory portal. You submit a repo of MCP servers and skills, it auto-validates and safety-scans the submission, and once you're live you get install and search analytics. It also supports MCP 2.0's stateless core, along with MCP Apps for interactive UI inside chat, and Enterprise Managed Auth for zero-touch OAuth. If you've built a GTM plugin, this is your distribution path.

And the Claude Marketplace launched, where enterprise buyers can spend part of their committed Anthropic budget on Claude-powered products. That's a procurement shortcut, and those are rare.

That's the diff. Watch Sacramento on Wednesday. If you ship something with those new plugin analytics, I'd like to hear what the numbers say. See you in ten days.
